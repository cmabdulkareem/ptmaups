Curriculum and subjects

Learning areas

The school's academic programme draws on broad learning areas that support literacy, reasoning, creativity and healthy development.

Language and literacy

Reading, writing, listening and speaking are treated as foundations for learning across the curriculum.

Mathematics and reasoning

Number work, problem-solving and logical thinking are developed through regular explanation and practice.

Environmental learning

Understanding the natural and social world through observation, discussion and guided inquiry.

Arts and expression

Drawing, craft, music and performance give students ways to observe, imagine and communicate.

Health and well-being

Physical activity, personal care and positive social habits support students' overall development.

Values and citizenship

Respect, responsibility and community awareness are part of the everyday life of the school.

This is a broad description of learning areas, not a confirmed subject list. The official curriculum will be published when supplied by the school.

Teaching and learning

A considered classroom approach

Good teaching makes the purpose of a lesson clear, builds understanding step by step and gives students enough practice to become confident.

At its best, the classroom is also a place of participation: students ask questions, explain their thinking and learn to listen to one another.

  • 01

    Clear explanation

    New ideas are introduced in a structured way, with examples and checks for understanding.

  • 02

    Guided practice

    Students practise with support before working more independently.

  • 03

    Useful feedback

    Feedback helps students see what they have achieved and what to work on next.

  • 04

    Active participation

    Discussion, group work and practical tasks give every learner a way into the lesson.
